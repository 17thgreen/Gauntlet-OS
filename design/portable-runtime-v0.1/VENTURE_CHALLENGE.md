# Venture Challenge operating mode

Date: 2026-09-13. Status: DESIGN DRAFT incorporating Logan's explicit follow-up requirements.

## Purpose

Give Gauntlet a challenge rather than a predetermined company: investigate broadly, choose a viable business, assemble its operating fleet, and create a path to revenue and self-sufficient operation.

Example challenge: a maximum of $500 may be made available. Identify and build a business that the fleet can substantially operate, prioritizing a credible short path to revenue, viable economics and potential to scale.

This is an operating-mode specification. The example does not fund a live mission, authorize spending, or start a business now.

## Required balance

Creative and commercial discretion belongs to the fleet within the mission. Human approval is required before spending money. Available capital is a ceiling, not an instruction to exhaust it.

Do not restrict discovery to a named niche, software-only products, previously discussed ventures, a fixed channel, or preselected business models. Existing projects and assets can be considered alongside new candidates. Existing holds remain binding unless Logan changes them.

“Self-sufficient” means demonstrated operational coverage within declared human dependencies. The fleet cannot represent itself as having legal identity, banking authority, physical capabilities or platform permissions it does not possess. Required owner actions are feasibility inputs, not reasons to hide operational gaps.

## Decision rights

| Decision/action | Fleet discretion |
|---|---|
| Discover business candidates and research markets | Yes, using available authorized sources and resources |
| Change the candidate shortlist, recommend a pivot or kill an idea | Yes, with evidence and retained decision history |
| Assemble roles and bounded specialist assignments | Yes, within the mission's authorized compute and tool envelope |
| Choose a preferred business | Yes; selection does not authorize a purchase |
| Draft code, pages, offers, workflows and operating procedures | Yes, within internal/reversible work scope |
| Decide proposed channel, pricing and experiment design | Yes; distinguish hypothesis from validated result |
| Spend money or incur a new paid commitment | Human approval required before execution |
| Publish, contact customers, submit offers or access accounts | Follow explicit mission authorization; absence of spend is not publication permission |
| Change approved purchase scope, recipient or price beyond envelope | New human approval required |
| Amend institution, fabricate evidence or bypass vetoes | No |

Human approval may cover an exact purchase or a clearly bounded group of expenses. There is no blanket “approved because total is under $500.” Approved envelopes must identify purposes, limits, vendors/target scope, validity and renewal behavior. Each charge must match an active human-issued envelope at dispatch.

Existing authorized model usage can continue within its defined allowance. New paid APIs, credits, subscriptions, ad spend, transaction fees or other commitments must be included in the applicable spending policy. Record owner-funded resources separately rather than presenting them as free economics.

## Dynamic fleet and hierarchy

The Conductor owns the challenge, composes assignments and makes the business selection within mandate. Specialists provide independent contributions before synthesis; no full-team debate by default. The roster below describes functions to cover, not a requirement for thirteen continuously active Bots.

| Function | Unique contribution | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Opportunity Scout | Broad discovery and overlooked opportunities | Cannot certify market demand |
| Demand Investigator | Buyer behavior, existing spend, timing and direct evidence | Distinguishes observed demand from model opinion |
| Distribution Architect | Reachable audiences, acquisition mechanisms, channel economics | No assumed access or fabricated CAC |
| Business Model Designer | Offer, pricing, delivery model and competitive differentiation | Retains assumptions and alternatives |
| Product/Software Builder | Prototype, integrations, technical delivery and tool gaps | Estimates are not executed acceptance tests |
| Operations Designer | Fulfillment, support, exceptions and recurring work | Must enumerate human dependencies |
| Economist | Contribution margin, cash timing, fixed/variable costs and downside | Does not turn guessed probabilities into precise EV |
| Capability/Access Reviewer | What the fleet can actually execute with current tools/accounts | Blocks infeasible capability claims |
| Architect | Combines independently supported components into coherent candidates | Each recombination gets its own consistency review |
| Examiner | Recalculates economics and evaluates tests/artifacts independently | Does not tune the chosen business to manufacture success |
| Prosecutor | Finds fatal assumptions, hidden labor and alternative explanations | Scoped objections with evidence and remediation |
| Treasurer/Risk Governor | Tracks reservations, approvals, exposure and contingent commitments | Cannot approve spending in place of the human |
| Archivist | Preserves candidates, rejected paths, evidence and operating lessons | Does not decide commercial validity |

Conductor may use short-lived workers instead of permanent Bots. The data governor function remains required for source integrity. Functions may share low-risk preparation resources only where institutional independence rules permit; different names on one broadly privileged process do not prove isolation.

## Workflow

### 1. Establish the challenge

Bind outcome, maximum capital, available tools/accounts, resource allowances, required human approvals and actual owner availability. Ask only for material missing constraints.

Create an asset inventory: reusable code, data rights, distribution, credibility, available integrations and fulfillment capacity. Do not mistake a repository for a working capability.

### 2. Discover broadly

Research multiple genuinely different mechanisms. Seek paying buyers, feasible delivery and a reachable route to those buyers. Consider services, products, information, automation and other legitimate models without a prescribed winner.

Each candidate includes the buyer, problem, offer, delivery, acquisition, economics, fleet coverage and most important uncertainty. Do not generate dozens of cosmetic variants solely to satisfy a count.

### 3. Challenge and narrow

Independent functions evaluate demand, distribution, implementation, operations and economics. Prosecutor challenges key assumptions; Examiner verifies calculations and measurement claims.

Use comparative evidence to narrow. Preserve rejected candidates and reasons. A preferred candidate may change as new evidence arrives.

### 4. Select and prepare

Conductor selects the best-supported current choice, explains tradeoffs and records what would reverse the decision. Unsupported business success remains a hypothesis.

Build useful free or already-authorized artifacts before requesting paid steps: prototype, offer, sample deliverable, workflow, acquisition plan and instrumentation. Preparation should make the spending decision concrete.

### 5. Request bounded spending

Present the exact spend request with a reviewable artifact and intended learning or delivery benefit. The human approves, rejects or modifies it. An unanswered request waits; it never becomes approval.

The team continues independent authorized preparation while a purchase is pending. It does not work around a rejected expense by creating a different paid commitment for the same action.

### 6. Run an authorized pilot

Operate only actions covered by the mission and spending envelopes. Record real customer responses, sales, fulfillment time, costs, failures and refunds. Separate simulated, owner-assisted and independently automated activity.

No purchase, customer contact or live deployment is authorized by this example document.

### 7. Continue, pivot, stop or scale

Conductor recommends the next move from observed outcomes. Preserve sunk costs without defending them. Recheck economics and operational capacity before expanding; additional spending still follows human approval.

A successful pilot is not automatically self-sufficient or scalable. Promotion of commercial claims uses the applicable domain's prospective evidence mapping and the existing institutional rules.

## Selection criteria

Evaluate these dimensions explicitly; avoid an arbitrary composite number:

| Dimension | Evidence/question |
|---|---|
| Time to first collected revenue | What exact steps, dependencies and customer actions precede payment? |
| Demand | Who already pays to address the problem, and what evidence supports this offer? |
| Distribution | How can this particular fleet reach qualified buyers? |
| Capital efficiency | How much must be committed before the first customer pays? |
| Unit economics | Price minus delivery, acquisition, fees, support and expected obligations |
| Implementation | Can the necessary product or service actually be delivered with available capabilities? |
| Fleet operability | What fraction of the workflow is demonstrably covered, including exceptions? |
| Human burden | Setup and recurring owner minutes; physical, relational and approval tasks |
| Scalability | Which resources grow with customers and where are the bottlenecks? |
| Repeatability | Retention, repeat demand or a reproducible acquisition mechanism |
| Downside | Unsold inventory, liabilities, refunds, renewals and failure recovery |
| Evidence quality | Verified facts versus estimates and unresolved assumptions |

First exclude infeasible or unauthorized paths. Compare remaining candidates on tradeoffs and ranges; use dominance comparisons where possible. When rankings depend on uncertain inputs, identify the cheapest informative test rather than inventing confidence.

Fast revenue, low human burden and scale potential can conflict. The Conductor should explain its choice and sensitivity to those tradeoffs. Broad creative control does not require ignoring economics.

## Required artifacts

- Challenge brief and mission specification.
- Asset/capability inventory.
- Candidate register with evidence and rejection reasons.
- Comparative assessment and selected-business decision.
- Offer, buyer and distribution specification.
- Working preparation artifacts and delivery plan.
- Operating fleet map, exception owners and human workload estimate.
- Cost/cashflow model separating estimates and observations.
- Spend requests and actual action receipts.
- Pilot measurement plan and outcome ledger.
- Continue/pivot/stop recommendation.

## Self-sufficiency milestones

| Milestone | Proof needed |
|---|---|
| Delivery-ready | Fleet can produce the promised output in a test |
| First revenue | Collected customer payment recorded, with delivery obligation visible |
| Repeatable operation | Multiple real deliveries with tracked quality, time and exceptions |
| Operational self-sufficiency | Defined recurring workflow covered within a declared human-intervention envelope over a specified observation period |
| Financial sustainability | Collected revenues cover the defined full operating costs and obligations over a specified period |
| Scalable operation | Capacity and economics withstand a measured increase in volume |

Periods, sample counts and intervention limits must be set prospectively for the selected business. One sale does not prove all six milestones.

## Asset discovery and unrestricted idea sourcing

Logan explicitly authorizes the mode's search scope to include his accessible GitHub repositories, previously built code and projects, preserved ideas, open-source repositories, and other online research. The fleet may reuse or recombine these assets, or originate a new business concept. It must not favor an existing project merely because work has already been invested.

At the beginning of a mission, inspect the actual accessible repository inventory. Search beyond the names already known in conversation. Use exact repository/commit/path references to support capability claims.

Treat existing projects as optional building materials:
- Reuse working code or data interfaces where demonstrated.
- Extract useful methods or design ideas from unfinished projects.
- Recombine capabilities across projects when coherent.
- Research wholly new opportunities when the evidence is stronger.
- Consider external open-source components and online methods alongside internal assets.

Distinguish provenance and authority. Access to a repository is not evidence that its software works, that embedded third-party assets are owned, or that operational deployment is permitted. Reading and proposing reuse does not authorize overwriting or deleting source projects. Preserve existing repos and frozen research while prototyping in an isolated workspace.

### Asset register

For every serious reuse candidate record:
- Asset ID, repository/URL, exact commit or retrieval time, relevant paths and original author.
- Capability claimed, actual inspection performed, tests executed, and current limitations.
- Code maturity: documented-only, stub, partial, tested component, or operationally demonstrated.
- License/terms, attribution, distribution limits, and unresolved rights for code, models, data and content.
- Dependencies, credentials, hosting needs, paid components and integration effort.
- Domain assumptions and evidence that they transfer.
- Proposed reuse approach: use unchanged, wrap, adapt, extract method, or decline.
- Alternative from-scratch approach and comparison of effort, risk and operating cost.

Publicly viewable code or content is not automatically licensed for copying or redistribution. Use online material as research evidence where appropriate; only import or republish material within applicable rights. Imported artifacts never gain authority to alter Gauntlet policy.

### Comparative selection

Candidate assessment must include the benefit of existing assets without treating sunk costs as future value. If an internal component lowers time to first revenue, identify the tested capability responsible. If it requires a substantial rebuild, include that cost rather than calling it free.

An existing held venture may supply a reusable method or component without activating the venture. If reuse conflicts with an explicit hold or owner restriction, flag the narrow conflict. Do not silently override it.

The preferred business can therefore be:
1. An improved use of an existing project.
2. A recombination of multiple internal capabilities.
3. A business assembled largely from compatible open-source components.
4. A new idea arising from independent market research.
5. A hybrid of these approaches.

These are permissible origins, not quotas.
